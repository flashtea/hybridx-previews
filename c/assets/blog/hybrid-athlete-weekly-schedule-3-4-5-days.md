The most common question I get isn't about sets or paces. It's "I have three days. What do I do with them?"

The number of days decides the shape of the week more than anything else. At three days, everything gets combined. At four, the sessions split. At five, you finally get the easy running volume that makes the rest work. Below are all three, day by day, and then the rules that survive every version.

One thing first: these are weeks for people who lift and run and want to keep doing both. If you're a lifter who runs twice a week to stay in shape, or a runner who lifts to stay injury-free, you'll want to lean the schedule toward your side. The layouts show where to lean.

## Three days: combined sessions

With three days, every session does two jobs. There's no such thing as a pure lifting day.

| Day | Session | Time |
|-----|---------|------|
| Mon | Lower strength (squat 4×5 at RPE 7, Romanian deadlift 3×6, split squat 3×8) then 20 min easy run straight after | 75 min |
| Wed | Quality run (10 min easy, 4×4 min at threshold, 2 min jog, 10 min easy) then upper strength (bench 4×5, row 3×8, pull-ups 3 sets, press 3×6) | 80 min |
| Sat | Long run, 60–75 min zone 2, then deadlift 3×3 at RPE 7 if you have the energy, or skip it | 75–90 min |

Weekly total: two lifts, three runs, about 20–25 km. Every session runs long, and every one of them pairs a hard thing with an easy thing — never two hard things.

The order inside each session follows one rule: whichever half matters more goes first. Monday is a lift with a jog after it. Wednesday is a run with a lift after it. Saturday is a run, and the deadlift is optional because it's the third hard thing in the week and the long run already took the legs. The [same-day rules](/blog/running-and-lifting-same-day) cover why the order works that way.

What three days gets you: strength maintained, running that gets steadily easier, and a body that isn't in pieces. What it doesn't get you: a fast race or a bigger squat. Be honest about that going in.

## Four days: the sessions split

At four days the doubles go away. Two lifts, one quality run, one long run.

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Mon | Lower strength — squat 4×5 at RPE 7, RDL 3×6, split squat 3×8 |
| Tue | Quality run — 5×3 min at threshold, 2 min jog |
| Thu | Upper strength + hinge — deadlift 3×3 at RPE 8, bench 4×5, pull-ups, row |
| Sat | Long run — 60–90 min zone 2 |

The full breakdown, with progression and the strength-first variant, is in [the 4-day template](/blog/hybrid-athlete-training-plan-4-day-week). The short version of the one compromise: squats Monday and intervals Tuesday sit next to each other because with four days something has to, and a squat capped at RPE 7 recovers inside 48 hours. If strength matters more, run the intervals Monday and squat Wednesday with a top set.

Weekly total: two lifts, two runs, 25–35 km. Enough to hold a deadlift and build to a comfortable half marathon over a year.

## Five days: easy volume appears

The fifth day is the most valuable day in hybrid training, because it's the first day that gets to be easy. Easy running is the base everything else stands on, and up to now there wasn't room for it.

**Balanced version (2 lifts, 3 runs):**

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Mon | Lower strength — squat 4×5 at RPE 7, RDL 3×6, split squat 3×8 |
| Tue | Easy run, 40–50 min zone 2 |
| Wed | Upper strength + hinge — deadlift 3×3, bench 4×5, pull-ups, row |
| Thu | Quality run — 5×3 min at threshold, or 20 min at tempo |
| Sat | Long run, 75–100 min zone 2 |

Look at where the hard days fall: Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday. The deadlift Wednesday to the quality run Thursday is the closest pairing, and it's a pull the day before a run rather than a squat — the hinge leaves the quads alone, which is what the intervals need. Deadlift on Friday would be the day before the long run, which is the one thing not to do.

**Strength-first version (3 lifts, 2 runs):**

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Mon | Squat — 5×3 at RPE 8, then press |
| Tue | Easy run, 40 min |
| Wed | Bench + upper volume |
| Fri | Deadlift — 3×3 at RPE 8, then pull-ups |
| Sun | Long run, 60–75 min zone 2 |

Three lifts, two runs, about 20 km. Running here is a supporting act — the long run keeps the aerobic base, the easy run keeps the habit, there's no quality session. Fine if the goal is a bigger total. If a race is on the calendar, use the balanced version.

Weekly total on the balanced version: 35–45 km and two lifts. This is the schedule where a marathon becomes possible; the [16-week marathon block for lifters](/blog/marathon-training-without-losing-strength) is a six-day week with the same skeleton.

## The rules that survive every version

Whatever the day count, these hold:

**Hard days need 48 hours from each other when they share tissue.** A heavy squat and an interval session are both leg days. A heavy squat and a bench session aren't. Spend your gaps on the pairs that need them — [leg day and running](/blog/leg-day-and-running-same-week) has the full list.

**The long run gets fresh legs.** Nothing heavy below the waist the day before it. Ever.

**Easy runs are easy.** Conversational, RPE 3–4, no exceptions. The moment the easy run becomes "medium," Monday's squats start failing and you'll blame the running. It isn't the running. It's the pace.

**Add 10% a week, not more.** To running minutes. Strength progresses by 2.5 kg when every set was RPE 7 or lower. Both feel slow. Both are the actual speed of adaptation.

**Cut back every fourth week.** Long run down 30%, quality session shortened, lifts at 2 sets. Not because you're tired — because you will be if you don't.

## FAQ

### How many days a week should a hybrid athlete train?

Four is the practical minimum for progress in both; five is where it gets comfortable because easy running finally has a day. Three works for maintenance with combined sessions. Six is for people with a race or a meet on the calendar and the recovery to match.

### Can I build muscle and run on three days a week?

You can keep it. Two lifts at RPE 7–8 on the big compounds hold muscle and strength; adding much needs more lifting volume than three days leaves room for. Eat at maintenance and expect to hold, not grow.

### Should lifting or running come first in the week?

Put the session that matters most on the freshest legs — for most people that's Monday after a rest day. Runners lead with the quality run; lifters lead with the squat. Then space the other hard days 48 hours apart and fill the gaps with easy running.
