Leg day and running argue over the same tissue. Quads, glutes, calves, and the 48 hours after you've loaded them. Get the spacing right and both improve. Get it wrong and you spend the week recovering from Monday.

I've done both versions. The first year I added running to powerlifting, my squat dropped 60 kg — [210 to 150](/blog/from-300kg-deadlift-to-340-marathon) — and a lot of that was scheduling, not physiology. Heavy squats Monday, intervals Tuesday, long run Saturday after Friday deadlifts. Every session was a fraction of what it should have been. This is the week I'd lay out now.

## The 48-hour rule

Heavy squats tank your running for 48–72 hours. Not the aerobic part — you can jog the next morning. The part that needs force: pushing off, holding pace, running downhill without your quads complaining. A hard run in that window is slower, feels worse, and at 85–90 kg body weight is where shins and knees start to speak up.

The rule, then, is about *which* running goes where:

| Run type | Day after leg day? | Minimum gap from heavy legs |
|----------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| Easy run, 30–50 min, zone 2 | Yes — it helps | None. Next morning is fine |
| Quality run (intervals, tempo, threshold) | No | 48 hours |
| Long run, 75 min or more | No | 48 hours, and never the day after a deadlift |
| Hill sprints or strides | No | 48 hours |

The easy run is the exception, and it's a useful one. A slow 30 minutes the morning after squats does more for the soreness than a rest day, as long as it stays slow. Coming from lifting you will want to push it because it feels too easy. Don't. The whole scheme depends on the easy run being easy — at RPE 3–4 you're not adding to the recovery bill, you're paying it down.

The reverse direction matters less. Lifting the day after a hard run is a smaller compromise than running hard the day after lifting: you can lower the bar and still hit RPE 7, and nobody gets injured squatting 5% lighter. So when the week forces a collision, put the run first and the lift second — the [same-day rules](/blog/running-and-lifting-same-day) go through the pairs one by one.

## Three weekly layouts that work

**One leg day, three runs.** The most common hybrid week, and the simplest to space.

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Mon | Leg day — squat 4×5 at RPE 7, RDL 3×6, split squat 3×8 |
| Tue | Easy run, 40 min |
| Thu | Quality run — 5×3 min at threshold |
| Sat | Long run, 75–90 min |
| Any other day | Upper body, wherever it fits |

Squats Monday, quality run Thursday: 72 hours. Long run Saturday: five days from the leg day. The easy run Tuesday sits right after leg day on purpose.

**Two leg days, three runs.** Squat and deadlift split across the week. This is the week most lifters actually want, and it's the one that goes wrong most often.

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Mon | Squat day — squat 4×5, split squat 3×8 |
| Tue | Easy run, 40 min |
| Wed | Quality run — 20 min at tempo |
| Thu | Deadlift day — deadlift 3×3 at RPE 8, RDL 2×6 |
| Fri | Easy run, 30 min, or off |
| Sun | Long run, 75–90 min |

The compromise is Tuesday–Wednesday: an easy run then a tempo, two days after a squat session. It works because Monday is fives at RPE 7, not a top set. The deadlift on Thursday is 72 hours from the long run. Deadlift Friday, long run Sunday, is the version people default to and it's the one that costs you both sessions — the pull is the lift that leaves the most in your hamstrings on Sunday morning.

**Leg day plus a hybrid circuit day.** If you're training for Hyrox or running sleds and wall balls once a week, that circuit *is* a leg day. Count it as one.

| Day | Session |
|-----|---------|
| Mon | Leg day — squat 3×5 at RPE 7, lunges 3×12 |
| Tue | Easy run, 40 min |
| Wed | Hybrid circuit — 5×(800 m run + 20 wall balls + 20 m sled) |
| Fri | Deadlift 3×3 + upper |
| Sun | Long run, 60–90 min |

Two things: the circuit is 48 hours from Monday's squats, and it's the week's quality run — there's no separate interval session, because that would be a third leg day in five days. [The Hyrox plan for lifters](/blog/hyrox-training-plan-for-lifters) builds a full eight weeks on this shape.

## Make leg day cheaper to recover from

The other lever, and the one lifters ignore: leg day doesn't have to cost 72 hours. A smaller, sharper session costs 36.

**Cap the RPE.** Fives at RPE 7 — three reps in reserve — recover inside two days. Fives at RPE 9 don't. The bar goes up more slowly this way, by 2.5 kg every couple of weeks instead of every week, and that's the price of running on the same legs.

**Cut the eccentric-heavy accessories in high-mileage weeks.** Walking lunges, Bulgarian split squats, anything with a deep stretch under load — these produce the most soreness per set of anything in the gym. In a base week with 25 km of running they're fine. In a 50 km week they're what make Thursday's tempo feel like a swim. Swap them for leg press or a belt squat, or drop them.

**Fewer sets, same weight.** Three working sets of the main lift at 80% holds strength about as well as five do, at a fraction of the recovery cost. When the running volume climbs past 45–50 km, drop to 2–3 sets and keep the load. [The interference effect](/blog/interference-effect-does-running-kill-gains) hits squat harder than deadlift precisely because the squat shares the most tissue with running, so the squat is the lift to trim first.

**Don't train to failure.** Ever, on legs, in a week with a hard run in it. The last grinding rep costs more than every rep before it.

## Signs the spacing is wrong

Easy runs that still feel heavy on day three. Pace drifting slower at the same heart rate. A squat that stalls for six weeks with no obvious reason. Shins that ache on the first kilometre and then settle. Every one of these is the recovery bill arriving.

The fix is almost never to drop something. It's to move something — usually the quality run, 24 hours further from leg day — or to cap Monday at RPE 7 instead of RPE 9. ## FAQ

### Should I run the day after leg day?

An easy run, yes — 30–45 minutes at a conversational pace helps the soreness more than a day off. A hard run, no. Intervals, tempo and long runs need 48 hours from heavy squats or deadlifts.

### How long after leg day can I run hard?

48 hours for most people after a moderate session (RPE 7, fives), 72 after a heavy or high-volume one. Deadlifts need the longer gap before a long run; the hamstrings are the last thing to come back.

### Can I do leg day and a long run in the same week?

Yes — that's the normal hybrid week. Put the leg day early (Monday), the long run late (Saturday or Sunday), and keep any deadlift at least 48 hours in front of the long run.
